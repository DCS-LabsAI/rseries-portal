- **Publication date of this page:** 2026-08-30

## Purpose

Zero-knowledge verification of a threshold-count statement: proving that a count satisfies a
threshold without revealing the underlying values. The published work is a Groth16 circuit, a
trusted-setup ceremony performed for it in May 2026, and a verifier deployed on Base mainnet.

**What is published and what is not are different circuits, and that distinction is the whole of
this page.** Everything public describes the **original** May 2026 circuit. The **repaired**
circuit is not published, and only its hash, constraint count, status and errata appear below —
never its internals.

## The warning that governs everything else here

The public R+4 repository ships the **original** May 2026 circuit. **Defects were found in that circuit
and repaired internally in August 2026.** The repaired circuit is **not published**, is **not frozen**,
and **has no trusted setup of any kind**.

> **Do not rely on the published circuit or the deployed verifier for production assurance.**

The erratum names the **class** of defect and nothing more. **No exploit detail and no proof artefact
demonstrating one is published, here or anywhere, and none will be while external review is
outstanding.**

### A blocker on the ceremony path, published because it gates the work

The identifier **`r4-threshold-count-v1` currently names two different constraint systems** — the
circuit the May 2026 ceremony transcript binds to, and the repaired circuit. **No
`r4-threshold-count-v2` exists.** One identifier naming two constraint systems is not a naming
inconvenience: a ceremony run against an ambiguous identifier produces a setup nobody can prove
belongs to a particular circuit. **The collision must be split, and C2.10 must be frozen, before
any ceremony can begin.** Neither has happened.

**A final freeze is not a formality and is not something this programme can grant itself.** It requires
independent external contributors, a beacon height pre-announced before contributions begin, and a
genuine window of at least fourteen days with dated attestations. None of those has happened. The
sealed state says only what is true.

## Deployed verifier

A Groth16 verifier for the **original** circuit is deployed on Base mainnet at
`0xabf8626c20e6bf21a9fdcd4e9f80c17ac8963209`, deploy transaction
`0x9df6edff61e9c63f25fbb1247a77a0f74cea128bb5a99427ea4bea902f01f53f`, block 46,343,838.

**Its source is not yet verified on the block explorer**, and verifying it is the single highest-value
open verification in the estate. The contract shows no on-chain transactions; because its verification
entry point is a view function, off-chain calls would leave none — **absence of transactions is
neither evidence of use nor evidence of non-use**, and this portal claims neither.

**This verifier covers the original circuit. It does not cover the repaired one.**
